About

Background & Expertise

I grew up in Madagascar, where the night sky isn't just beautiful — it's overwhelming. That feeling of standing under a canopy of stars and sensing that you are part of something much larger than yourself: that's what started everything.

In 2015, I founded Haikintana, a Malagasy word for "astronomy", with a simple idea: to build a community where people passionate about astronomy and space could come together, learn, and grow. What began as a gathering of enthusiasts grew into something much bigger: public festivals, school outreach programmes, conferences, and eventually Madagascar's first operational astronomical observatory, built through international partnerships with French research institutions.

Along the way, I represented Haikintana at international forums, worked with the IAU, the French Astronomical Society, and institutions across Europe and Africa. Living in France for the past nine years gave me a new perspective, not just on what the space sector does, but on why it does it.

That question, why, became central to everything. Too often, space exploration advances because it is technically possible, without enough reflection on whether it is strategically wise, equitable, or sustainable. I want to be part of the conversation that asks those harder questions: How do we build space cooperation frameworks that are truly inclusive? How do we ensure that space benefits humanity as a whole, not just the nations that got there first?

That's what brought me to the International Space University as a CNES Scholar — and the work I want to dedicate my career to.
